SQL Database Design: Connecting Tables to Avoid Data Duplication

SQL, Database, Normalization, Tables, Relationships, Data Duplication, MusicApplication

This lecture explains how to design databases using multiple tables to manage complex data relationships. It emphasizes the fundamental principle of avoiding data duplication by ensuring string information appears only once in the database structure. The speaker describes how database normalization techniques help organize data across tables while maintaining efficient performance and reducing redundancy.

The lecture demonstrates this through a music management application example. Starting with tracks as the foundational data unit, it shows how to separate attributes like title, rating, length, and play count into a tracks table. Remaining elements—artists, albums, and genres—are then modeled into distinct tables to eliminate vertical duplication. This approach creates relationships between tables, such as tracks belonging to albums that belong to artists, while ensuring genre information stays connected to individual tracks rather than albums.
